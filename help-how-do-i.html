Game-Maker: Sandbox Edition Guide

How do I…?

The Starters are small games that each show one idea: walking and jumping, keys and doors, monsters, and rooms joined by doors. Each question below is answered by one level. Play this level starts the game at it. See how it’s made opens its map at the spot, to look at only, with a How this level is made button for the manual’s page about it. To change something, choose Edit a copy on the bar above the map.

Moving and jumping

The hero walks with the arrow keys and jumps with Space. A wall two blocks high is too tall to walk through, so the level asks for a jump.

Try it: First Steps, level 1.

Gravity

A block can pull the hero down. Walk off an edge and the pull of the sky block does the rest.

Try it: First Steps, level 1.

Ladders

A ladder is a block with no pull and no solid side, so Up lifts the hero.

Try it: First Steps, level 2.

Keys and doors

A key adds one to a counter, and a locked door spends one from it.

Try it: Keys and Doors, level 1.

Counters

A door can cost two keys, and a second counter gives a second kind of key.

Try it: Keys and Doors, levels 2 and 3.

Monsters

A monster walks a short path of corners, back and forth.

Try it: Meet the Monsters, level 1.

Hurting

A monster’s picture can take hit points away. Another can be popped by touching it.

Try it: Meet the Monsters, levels 1 and 2.

Scene links and exits

A door, or an open archway in a room's wall, is a link to another scene.

Try it: Room to Room, levels 1 and 3.

Treasure and score

A coin block adds points to the score, one can be worth more, and a hazard can take points away.

Try it: Coins and Score.

Hit points and lives

The hero starts with hit points, a heart gives some back, and running out costs a life.

Try it: Health and Hearts.

Hazards

A block can hurt every time or only once, and a pool can cost a life.

Try it: Watch Your Step.

Warning before it happens

A hazard that blinks first gives the player a fair chance, and can ring again and again.

Try it: Warning First.

Powers that run out

A block gives a power: a higher jump or quicker feet, for a while.

Try it: Power Up.

Throwing and weapons

A key throws a star that flies a set distance; a pouch can hold other things to throw.

Try it: Throw It and Many Weapons.

Who beats whom

One monster can be stronger than another, and one can pop another by touching it.

Try it: Who Wins?

Bosses

A boss takes several hits, and can change its look and speed as it is hurt.

Try it: Big Bug and Three Phases.

Following, flocking and watching

A monster can follow the hero, fly in formation, or sweep a light back and forth.

Try it: Follow Me, Birds of a Feather and Don’t Get Seen.

Monsters that come and go

A block can hatch a monster, and a monster can vanish or change after a while.

Try it: Seeds and Nests, and Here Today.

Popping numbers

A touched monster can turn into a number that floats up and goes away.

Try it: Score Popups.

Wind, ice and springs

Blocks can slide, carry or lift the hero, and wind can warn before it blows.

Try it: Slippery and Bouncy and Gusts.

Speed and dashes

The hero can pick up speed while a key is held, glide on, or dash once for each press.

Try it: Slide and Drift and Kit’s Moves.

Water

Water can pull the hero sideways without hurting, and the hero can float.

Try it: Under the Surface.

Looking down and gravity off

Turn the pull off for a level that looks down from above.

Try it: Gravity On, Gravity Off, level 2.

Digging and breaking

Some blocks can be dug through or broken from any side, and leave something behind.

Try it: Dig It and Smash It.

Levers, gates and puzzles

A lever can make a bridge, a gate can need three tokens, and a puzzle can need two things at once.

Try it: Switches, Three Tokens and Both Together.

Secret walls

A wall can look solid and let the hero through, or look secret and stay solid.

Try it: Looks Like a Wall.

Clocks and countdowns

A bar can show time running out, and a number can count down to a door.

Try it: Beat the Clock and Countdown.

Signs and talk

A sign shows words, and a character talks when the hero walks into them.

Try it: Say Something and Everybody Talks.

Acting scenes

An actor can walk in, say hello and walk out, one act turning into the next.

Try it: The Show.

Pictures and cartoons

A picture or cartoon can play before or after a level.

Try it: Pictures and Cartoons.

Songs and sounds

A room can play its own song, and a pickup can play its own sound.

Try it: Sounds and Music.

Day and night

The palette can change when the player walks somewhere, to make a sunset in steps.

Try it: Day and Night.

Choosing a hero and driving

A door can pick the hero, and a level can give the hero another character, a car, say.

Try it: Who Do You Want to Be? and Take the Wheel.

Scrolling

A level can stay on one screen or scroll to follow the hero.

Try it: Scroll or Stay.

Rooms on one map

Several rooms can share a map, with doors that land in another room.

Try it: Rooms on One Map.

Bigger pictures

One picture can span several blocks, and a road can run away to the horizon.

Try it: 150 Blocks and To the Horizon.

Near and far

A level can have a near scene and a far one, drawn smaller and paler, with a trail between them.

Try it: Near and Far.

Putting a game together

Levels join into one game with doors, coins, a hazard and a key.

Try it: Your First Game.
