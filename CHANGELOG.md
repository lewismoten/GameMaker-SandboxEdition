# Changelog

Changes to the Game-Maker: Sandbox Edition site, released as `pages-v*` tags. Each package has its own
changelog: [engine](web/packages/engine/CHANGELOG.md), [archives](web/packages/archives/CHANGELOG.md) and
[tools](web/packages/tools/CHANGELOG.md). The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

## [Unreleased]

The 1994 desk gets a fuller PC and a box of feelies, the editors become easier on touch screens and keyboards,
the Phone of the Future kit grows, and Tune-Up's rubber chicken has a story to finish.

### Added

- Looks Like a Wall, a Starter for the Wiring and Puzzles group: a wall and a picture of a wall can be drawn exactly alike, and only the block's Solid setting says which stops Kit. A touch can turn a block into a cracked one and that into chips, and coins showing in a wall hint at a secret. Three levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- The Show, a Starter for the Foes and Bosses group about plays made of monsters: two friends wait behind a stage curtain and, when the level starts, each peeks out, walks in, says hello and walks off, every act a monster that becomes the next one. Then a cameo waves hello once and is gone. A play never waits for the player, so an encore door at the start loads the level again. Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Follow Me, a Starter for the Foes and Bosses group: Puff, a little winged pup, flies straight after Kit (a monster's Movement set to chase the character, at a speed of 2). Run ahead and Puff falls behind; stop and it catches up. In the second level Kit stands on a ledge and Puff, which plans no way around, waits under it until he steps off. Two levels, with signs, a "How this level is made" page each and a page on what a follower cannot do.
- Birds of a Feather, a Starter for the Foes and Bosses group: five birds fly in a V over Kit and keep that shape, because they share one path and one speed (a monster's Movement set to follow its path). In the second level two of them are faster and the V comes apart. Two levels, with signs, a "How this level is made" page each and a page on what a flock cannot do.
- Both Together, a Starter for the Wiring and Puzzles group: an AND made of monster power. A spark runs along a wire in the sky and two dials hang on it. A dial that is off is stronger than the spark and stops it; one that is on is equal and lets it through; only a spark that passes every dial lights the lamp, and the lamp's light opens the gate. Two levels (two dials, then three with one up a ladder), with signs, a "How this level is made" page each and a page on what it cannot do (no OR, no NOT).
- Slide and Drift, a Starter for the Game Feel group: every step Kit takes adds to his speed instead of moving him (the Momentum setting on a character sequence). In level 1 he gets faster while a key is held, glides on after he lets go and turns around with the other key; in level 2 a hop made at speed carries across three gaps a standing hop cannot. Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each, and a page on what Game-Maker cannot do (no top speed, no braking key, a fixed rate of slowing).
- Every game's download ZIP and DOS folder now has a short name of up to 8 letters (KEYSDOOR.ZIP, RECKON.ZIP), so DOS shows it whole instead of as `KEYS-A~1`. The web addresses still use the game's name, and the old ZIP links keep working.
- A saved game with damaged parts (a cut-short or wrong-type file, a bad notes file, a file its game names that is gone) still opens: every good file loads, a note lists what is broken in plain words with Replace and Drop buttons, and the damaged save is kept whole as a "damaged copy" so nothing is overwritten. A browser that won't open its game storage says so instead of showing a blank page.
- When something breaks, a small notice offers Copy error details (the message, the build, the page, the browser's name and the screen size) and Dismiss. It copies only when you press it and sends nothing.
- The Starters tab groups its games the way the Bizarre tab groups its songs, each group with a heading and a line about it (The Basics today), and the "How do I…?" list says which group a question's game is in.
- Throw It, a Starter for the Game Feel group: press Enter and Kit throws a gold star the way he last walked. It pops a mint green gumdrop, falls short if you stand too far away, and clears a grump out of a narrow brick tunnel. Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Say Something, the first Starter of the Telling a Story group, with its own stage-and-curtains title: signs of three sizes and three friends who talk when Kit walks into them (a picture with a speech bubble, then pages of words, then back in the room). Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Day and Night, a Starter for the Telling a Story group: the same map in two palettes. Kit goes in a house's door and comes out in the very same room at night, where the sun is a moon, the windows glow and the stars the day hid are out. A second level is a street with two gates, and the sun sets through three palettes as he walks. The manual says plainly how a scene picks its palette, why each scene fades in from black, and what Game-Maker cannot do (no more than 256 colors, and the block art is shared). Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Countdown, a Starter for the Putting It Together group: a number that counts down. A big digit on a brick building counts 9 to 0 and the door under it opens at 0, so Kit may go in. Two digits count 99 to 00 in a closed room and at 00 the floor flashes and drops away onto a soft mat, so Kit is taken out. The manual says plainly how a digit is a row of blocks that turn into the next number, and what Game-Maker cannot do (no number readout). Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Warning First, a Starter for the Putting It Together group: things that warn you before they happen. Three fog vents in the ground rumble, then puff a small sting, then rest, and take turns; two plank bridges over a soft pit flicker, vanish and come back. The manual says plainly that Game-Maker cannot start a warning when you do something, and shows how a puff stings only once. Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Gusts, a Starter for the Game Feel group: wind that changes over time. In the first level the leaves in a shaft of air flutter, then a gust blows up and lifts Kit to a ledge too high to jump to. In the second, gusts blow along a tunnel and push him back, windsocks on the hill droop, twitch and stream in step with the wind, and two little shelters are a place to wait. The manual says plainly that a gust is a ring of blocks with fixed times, so it cannot be random. Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Switches, a Starter for the Putting It Together group, with its own blueprint-and-workbench title: walk into a lever and it puts planks on a counter, and the dotted planks over a pool spend one each as Kit steps on them and turn solid. A red lever pays for red planks and a blue lever for blue, and the wrong lever leaves you in the water. Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Smash It, a Starter for The Basics: walk into a crate and it turns to rubble, and only the crates Kit touches break. A coin that turns into itself pays again and again until it is changed to turn into air, and a spinning gem is four blocks in a loop that each have to turn to air. Three levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Scroll or Stay, the first Starter of a new group, Bigger Worlds (with its own old-atlas title look): one level is a single room that fits the screen exactly and never scrolls, and the next is a long walk where the picture follows Kit. Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Who Do You Want to Be?, a Starter for Game Feel: a scene names the character file it loads, so two doors can lead to the same level with two different heroes. Fast Feet runs quickly and jumps as Kit does; Big Hops walks slowly, jumps six blocks and can reach the coins up high. Two levels, with signs, a "How this level is made" page each and a page on what Game-Maker cannot do (a pick is not remembered between scenes).
- Kit's Moves, a Starter for Game Feel: what a key does while it is held. Space hops once however long it is held, Up keeps lifting Kit up a tall cliff for as long as it is down, and Enter dashes once for each press, so a wide gap takes press after press. Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Many Weapons, a Starter for Game Feel: one key, many weapons. Enter throws a flame if Kit has one, a star if he has one, and shrugs when he has neither, and a pouch of each fills the counters. A star pops a mint green gumdrop but cannot melt a frosty grump, and a flame can. Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Under the Surface, a Starter for Game Feel: water is a block with a gravity setting, and nothing in it hurts. A creek pulls Kit left and carries him back to the bank, a deep pool pulls up so he floats, and a tall one pulls up and left to lift him to a ledge. Three levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Dig It, a Starter for The Basics: soil is a block that turns into a tunnel block when it is touched, so Kit walks straight into a hill and out the other side, and the standing soil under his feet gives way and he sinks. A rock does not dig, gold flecks pay when dug, and a lane of spikes on the surface is passed by tunneling under it and climbing a ladder out. Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- The disk box on the desk holds a Game-Maker Sample Disk with the first three small games, each in its own folder (A:\FIRSTSTP, KEYSDOOR, MONSTERS), as many as fit a 1.44 MB diskette. Game-Maker loads them to play or design, and the Owner's Guide names the folders.
- The CD-ROM on the desk (D:) now has a SAMPLES folder with every Starter in its own 8.3 folder (D:\SAMPLES\FIRSTSTP, KEYSDOOR, MONSTERS, ROOMS, COINS, HAZARDS, POWERUP, THROWIT), and a longer README.TXT. Game-Maker plays them from the CD, or copies one to C:\GM to change it.
- The 1994 Owner's Guide on the desk lists six of the small sample games on the Sample CD, three of them also on the Sample Disk (First Steps, Keys and Doors, Meet the Monsters, Room to Room, Coins and Score, Watch Your Step), one idea each, and says how to open one in the designers.
- Health and Hearts, a Starter for the Game Feel group: Kit starts with 30 hit points. A spike takes ten, a heart gives ten back, and at zero a life is gone and the room starts again. A smiling face adds a life, and F2 shows the numbers. Three levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Slippery and Bouncy, a Starter for the Game Feel group: gravity as a tool. Ice drags Kit along and he slides on after he lets go, a belt carries him down its length, a spring pad bounces him onto a ledge and a fan's wind lifts him up through a one-way shelf to the door. Three levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Sounds and Music, a Starter for the Telling a Story group: three short rooms, one with a bouncy song, one with a waltz and one with no song at all. Coins, money bags, hearts and spikes each play a sound of their own, and jumping has a beep too. Signs say how, each level has a "How this level is made" page, and the manual says plainly what the engine can't do (a block can't start a song, only one sound plays at a time).
- Beat the Clock, the first Starter for the Putting It Together group: a bar of five green pieces goes out one by one, and when it is dark the gate in the brick wall shuts, so run for the exit or walk into the clock door and start again. The second level's clock starts over by itself. A clock here is a block that turns into another block after some ticks. Two levels, with signs, a "How this level is made" page each and a page on what Game-Maker cannot do.
- Gravity On, Gravity Off, a Starter for the Game Feel group: the same little place twice. Level 1 is a side view where gravity pulls Kit down, so he jumps a wall and falls off a ledge. Level 2 is the same scene from above with gravity off, and Kit walks up, down, left and right and goes around the wall. Its manual shows that the difference is one block setting, Gravity pulls.
- Who Wins?, the first Starter of a new Foes and Bosses group, with a red-and-gold arena title: every monster has a power, and when two touch the weaker one is gone. A big red gumdrop pops two small ones that block a tunnel, then Kit's star (power 140) pops a small gumdrop and a grump but is used up on the big one. Two levels, with signs, a "How this level is made" page each and a page on what Game-Maker cannot do.
- Three Phases, a Foes and Bosses Starter about a boss that changes as it is hurt: Big Bug paces a long tunnel, turns into a quicker orange bug at half, and in level 2 a tiny purple one for the last hit. Each phase is its own monster with its own picture and path. Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Here Today, a Foes and Bosses Starter about things that do not last: a bubble blocks a tunnel and pops on its own after a set time, and a sleepy gumdrop in the next tunnel wakes up and becomes another monster that Kit can walk through. Two levels, with signs, a "How this level is made" page each and a page on what Game-Maker cannot do (the clock cannot start on a trigger).
- Three Tokens, the first Starter of a new Wiring and Puzzles group, with a green circuit-board title: purple tokens add to a count, and a gate opens only when the count can pay its price. The first gate takes three tokens (with two it stays shut and keeps them), and in the second level the gate is the exit. Two levels, with signs, a "How this level is made" page each and a page on what Game-Maker cannot do.
- Take the Wheel, a Game Feel Starter about changing who you are: Kit walks and jumps a bird in the road, steps into a little kart, and the next level names a different character file, so the same Kit drives fast, cannot jump and honks to clear the road. Two levels, with signs, a "How this level is made" page each and a page on what Game-Maker cannot do (a character cannot change inside a scene).
- Score Popups, a Telling a Story Starter about the numbers that rise when you score: a coin that is a monster pays 100 and becomes a blinking 100 that floats up and is gone, a gem and a box have numbers of their own, and one number is built wrong so it never leaves. Two levels, with signs, a "How this level is made" page each and a page on what Game-Maker cannot do (it cannot draw a number, so every number is a picture).
- Everybody Talks, the first Starter of the Showcase Tricks group, with a night-and-neon title: a whole talk in one picture. Level 1 is a talk of two (Kit and Pip); level 2 is a group of four, across two cards because a card holds 16 rows. Two levels, with signs, a "How this level is made" page each and a page on what Game-Maker cannot do (the words cannot name a speaker, and they are all one color).
- To the Horizon, a Starter for the Showcase Tricks group: how a big game drives to the horizon. Two levels, with signs, a "How this level is made" page each and a page on what it cannot do.
- Your First Game, the last Starter, in the Putting It Together group: three short levels that use a little of everything (a jump over a wall, coins and a score, a friendly gumdrop, spikes, a key for a locked door), with a title, signs in the levels and an ending. - Pictures and Cartoons, a Starter for the Telling a Story group: a picture comes up before each of three short levels, another as Kit leaves the first, and a little cartoon (a seed, the sun, a daisy opening, Kit cheering) plays between the second and the third. Signs, a "How this level is made" page each and a page on what Game-Maker cannot do (sizes, colors, silence, no skipping a cartoon).
- Big Bug, the first Starter of the new Foes and Bosses group, with its own red-and-gold arena title: a boss that takes five hits. Big Bug sits in a narrow way, a gold star does not stop it, and Kit throws five. In level 1 you count; in level 2 its shell has five lights and one goes out per hit. Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Don't Get Seen, a Foes and Bosses Starter about a light that cannot look: a friendly lamp hangs from a rail and walks back and forth, and anyone it touches loses hit points. Two levels (one light, then two at different speeds), with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- Rooms on One Map, the first Starter of the new Bigger Worlds group, with its own old-atlas title: four rooms in four corners of one map, each a scene that loads the same map file with scrolling off and a different landing cell, joined by doors that land in other cells (the second level lands every door on one cell). Two levels, with signs and a "How this level is made" page each.
- 150 Blocks, a Bigger Worlds Starter about the 150 blocks a game gets: a big picture is a group of blocks cut like tiles, a block used again costs nothing more, and a picture is not solid unless its blocks say so. - Seeds and Nests, a Starter for The Basics group: a block can hatch a monster. - Kit, the hero of the Starter games, joins the Cast page with a new Starters section. - A "How do I…?" list on the Starters tab and in the Guide: search a question ("How do I make a door that needs a key?"), then choose Play this level, which starts the game at that level, or See how it's made, which opens its map in the read-only editor at the right spot with a button for the manual's "How this level is made" page. The Guide's new "How do I…?" section and its Try it boxes use the same buttons. A new Starter adds its questions with a `howDoI` list in its manual.json.
- "About this game" (Overview, Tools): title, author, company, year, a short description, story, genre, website, license and credits, kept with the project but never in the game's files. The DOS download gets a FILE_ID.DIZ made from them (10 lines of 45 characters, shown live as you type), and importing a zip with a FILE_ID.DIZ fills them in. The one-page web download adds a visible, folded "Inside this game" section with the game's pictures (each stored once, as an image the page reads back), its text screens as real text, a preview of each animation and sound, and the credits, plus structured data for search engines. The Download dialog has "Include animation and sound previews" (on by default, remembered per game) and says how much page size they add. A starter's title is shown from its animation's first frame.
- The map editor remembers each map's zoom and scroll spot, in every game (read-only ones too): go to another map and back, or reload, and it is as you left it. A map you have not looked at before opens at the zoom of the last map you looked at, centered on where the character enters. Kept only in this browser (up to 200 maps), never in game files or backups.
- Importing a game shows a panel the moment you pick or drop the file: its name, a progress bar with the step (reading, unpacking, finding the game, extracting, opening), the file being extracted (n of m), a Cancel button, and a message if it fails. A second pick waits until the first import ends. The exported one-page game, opened where scripts don't run (such as a Files preview on iPhone), now shows a dark page with the game's title screen and a note on how to open it in a browser to play.
- A "Uses" line beside "Used by": a scene lists the files it is made of and a map its block sets and monsters, each a link that keeps Back and the breadcrumb working. A link to the open game with another scene now selects that scene (and Back and Forward step through them), and a page you go Back to waits up to 3 seconds for its content before giving up on its scroll spot.
- A breadcrumb above every open file (Overview › Maps › bus2.map) and a Back to the file you came from. "Used by" links now open the scene they name, and Back to an Overview page lands where you left it.

### Changed

- Pip, the friend in Say Something and Everybody Talks, has a new look of his own: a bee-striped sweater, round glasses and a headband with two bobbles, with no hat and no beard.

- Home: the Gameware and Shareware tabs are now one CD tab, Gameware first and then Shareware, each by maker. Old Gameware and Shareware links open it, and every game is still on the desk, in DOS, in Credits and in search.
- The game list that the home page and the DOS page load is smaller again: each Starter's blurb, label and box art, and every original game's controls and player look, come with it only when the game is opened, so more Starters can be added without slowing those pages.
- The desk loads less as games are added: each box on the wall shelf is drawn when its slot is near the screen, its spine is a small picture at the size it stands (no separate request per box), and a box standing spine out no longer makes its front under black light.
- On the Starters tab the "How do I…?" list is one button that opens a dialog with a search box (focused), a count of the matches and the questions, each with Play this level and See how it's made. The list scrolls inside the dialog, with a scrollbar you can see, so the tab no longer loses a third of its height to it and there is no Show all button to miss. Closing the dialog (Esc or Close) puts the keyboard back on the button: 1 Tab stop instead of 14. The Guide's index is unchanged.
- The Starters tab and its "How do I…?" list show the games in learning order, First Steps first, not A to Z.

- Pictures redrawn after a "what would a child point out?" pass: the sword on I Screamed, She Smirked's title is a fat orange foam sword; the Cleanse Me, O Loofah sponge is a golden, fuzzy, goofy-faced bath monster with no horns; Veni, Vidi, Risi's Rufus is plainly a dog; Tippy in KEEP IT BRIEF!'s The Scissor Situation card sits up crying; and Final Curtain's umbrella man tips his hat.
- Map editor on iPhone: two fingers over the map pinch the map, not Safari's page, and never leave a stray stroke; a Zoom tool (tap to zoom in) and visible + and − buttons beside the zoom menu; the block highlighter is now "Find block".

- Editors: 44 px touch targets throughout, one Tab stop with arrow keys for the file list, slide tray and path tabs, and a small Saved pill where the file list folds away.
- PC games ask for setup on PLAYGAME's own Configure screen, as the DOS program does.
- Box viewer on a phone: the Feelies strip sits above the disks and manual, and dragging always turns the model.
- Header: Games moves into the menu on 390 px phones.
- Music: a Games row in the sound settings scales everything a game makes; Voice also covers phone callers.
- The Optimize dialog works on look-only games by making a copy in My Games, and its picture color tables shrink to the smallest power of two that holds the colors drawn.
- Games with identical palettes share one file, and the cartoons' FLI clips are smaller; Depth's full-screen stills render faster.
- Opening a game and the shelf are faster: zips are peeked for the files asked for, four zips build at a time, and the home page lets go of what it watches.
- Games revised: Final Curtain 1.5, Teddy's Little Biggest Adventure 1.6, Quest for the Round Tuit 1.5, Tune-Up's Beltway and Timing Beat, with visible doorways at the ends of several levels.
- The Starters have no invisible walls: each room ends in a hedge you can see, and a check fails if a solid block is ever drawn as sky. Room to Room's second room now leaves by an open archway with a THIS WAY OUT sign instead of an invisible edge.
