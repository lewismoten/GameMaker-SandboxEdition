# Changelog

Changes to the Game-Maker: Sandbox Edition site, released as `pages-v*` tags. Each package has its own
changelog: [engine](web/packages/engine/CHANGELOG.md), [archives](web/packages/archives/CHANGELOG.md) and
[tools](web/packages/tools/CHANGELOG.md). The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

## [Unreleased]

## [3.5.0] - 2026-11-11

The wrong-number die, the cryptex and the Snowbound puzzle box assemble in 3D on the desk, In The Year 2000! reaches 1.8, the map editor and home get finger-sized controls, dialogs fit small and sideways phones, and the DOS page, Library and home download less.

### Added

- Watch the Plowed Street puzzle box and Teleport-A-Phone cryptex assemble one cut part at a time on the desk's Feelies shelf, and the Wrong-Number die fold in 3D.
- Dated Game-Maker history notes on user-made games, shareware catalogs and magazine discovery.

### Changed

- The cryptex kit has hollow rings, IDs on hidden faces and a 3D assembly that ends with setting your own code.
- The DOS page, the Library and home download less on their first load.
- On touch screens the map editor's controls and the Level check panel are 44 px tall.

### Fixed

- Dialogs fit 360 by 740 phones and phones held sideways, with their actions in view.
- Leaving the block editor, a game's box poster, the walkthrough player and the Level Maps viewer frees what they used.
- In The Year 2000! 1.8: the CitiCar needs the Zapper, the pages and Grandpa's keys, and the backyard pool looks like a pool.
- Pocket Player SD packs carry each game's DOS menu picture, credits and Top Ten backdrop.

Full list: [changes/release/pages/3.5.0.md](https://github.com/lewismoten/GameMaker-SandboxEdition/blob/pages-v3.5.0/changes/release/pages/3.5.0.md)

## [3.4.0] - 2026-11-09

Three new laser and paper kits on the box shelf (the Snowbound puzzle box, the In The Year 2000! cryptex and a paper THE CHEZ), Pocket Player opens a game's Details first, Rain on the Path 1.2, and better search descriptions.

### Added

- A laser-cut puzzle box for Snowbound on its box shelf: the lid is a street map, and sliding the plow along the plowed streets to LaVale Mall opens the drawer (SVG sheets for a Glowforge, previews and assembly steps).
- A laser-cut cryptex for In The Year 2000! on its box shelf: four stepped rings of digits, dial the year and the hollow core slides out, and the combination can be changed (SVG sheets for a Glowforge, previews and assembly steps).
- A fold-up paper model of THE CHEZ on the Tune-Up box shelf: a Glowforge sheet (cardstock, with scored folds and cut windows and headlights) and color print pages for Letter or A4.
- The web player page (web-player.html and its try-it copy) has its own title, description, address and sharing picture, a heading without JavaScript, and a line in the sitemap.

### Changed

- Pocket Player: tapping a game opens its Details (Back, Favorite, Play) instead of starting it, and Settings, Controls, Practice has a "Hold it" button that shows the "How to hold it" hands again.
- Pocket Player's first run says how to finish ("Touch the pad or Jump, then tap Done"), and its small hint text is a lighter gray, easier to read at arm's length.
- The Pocket Player firmware now has its own public repository, and the Pocket Player download page and card help point there.
- Rain on the Path 1.2: the cottage's gutter now runs into a downspout pipe down the wall, pouring into the rain barrel with a splash at its base.
- Each art course page now has its own search and link-preview description, not the contents page's.

### Fixed

- The credits screens of two games now name both AI models that made them, Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Claude Opus 5.5.

Full list: [changes/release/pages/3.4.0.md](https://github.com/lewismoten/GameMaker-SandboxEdition/blob/pages-v3.4.0/changes/release/pages/3.4.0.md)

## [3.3.0] - 2026-11-09

Four games grow: The Mountain Moved Next Door (3.2, twenty-four levels, about an hour), Pass the Pie (3.3, thirteen levels, inked
art), Lucky Cat (3.1, fifteen levels) and The Thank-You Parade (3.2, fifteen levels). The Library gets seven art courses and
pauses its music while a video plays, Pocket Player is easier to pick up, and two small desk and phone fixes come along.

### Added

- Art courses (#/art-courses): seven courses on drawing a game's art, from blocks and palettes to moving characters and title screens, on the Library shelf.
- DOS downloads include INSTALL.BAT and plain installation instructions for hard disks and floppy copies, with an optional 1.44 MB disk-layout script and numbered disk prompts.

### Changed

- The Mountain Moved Next Door 3.2: a bolder printed look with a palette for each time of day, and twenty-four levels (eleven new) that run about an hour from dawn to the next sunrise, among them a clock-tower Bell Loft.
- Pass the Pie 3.3: rebuilt levels, inked halftone art, new music and sound effects, a three-part fight with Big Gobble and six new levels, thirteen in all.
- Lucky Cat 3.1: seven new levels, 15 in all, including a mirror level that runs right to left and a Night Market where the shutters slam at the 13th chime.
- The Thank-You Parade 3.2 grows from seven levels to fifteen, with a page of story after every level card, a Cannon Truck boss, a cartoon between acts and two new songs.
- The Library's background music pauses while a video or walkthrough is open, and the AV cart's TV looks like a 1990s set.
- Pocket Player is easier to pick up: a hint to tap a game again to play it, grayed buttons that would do nothing, a second tap to restart, redrawn hand pictures and a small character in Try the controls.

### Fixed

- The phone's Hint Giant says Gwinn, THE CHEZ and the Round Tuit more clearly in the clips that name them.
- On the 1994 desk, Scoot's speech bubble now shows in front of the PC instead of being cut off behind it.
- Pocket Player returns to the game list, with a short message, when a scene card can't be read, instead of drawing a partly loaded level.

## [3.2.0] - 2026-11-07

Specials arrive: limited-time games that come out only on their day or season, on the home page and on the 1994 desk.
106 Starters, small games that each teach one idea, fill a new tab and the desk's Sample CD, and the Pocket Player plays
games from a microSD card on two small touch-screen boards. The desk gets a fuller PC and a disk box with dividers, the
editors are easier on phones, touch screens and keyboards, and pages and the offline copy download less.

### Added

- Specials: limited-time games that come out only on their day or season, in a new Special tab on the home page and on the 1994 desk. Two new ones: The Mountain Moved Next Door (the three days after Thanksgiving) and The Noodle Observatory (Monkey Day, December 14).
- A Starters tab with 106 Starters, small games that each teach one idea, with signs in their levels, a 32-bar song each, and two achievements: Show and tell and Honor roll.
- The Pocket Player, a small touch-screen board (3.5 inch or 2.8 inch) set up from the site's install page, with a game picker and title search. The Download chooser can save a game as one .gmp file for its microSD card.
- The desk PC runs RSD's batch files as a real PC did, and the desk's disk box files its floppies behind index-card dividers.
- Phone of the Future: the Junior Lab case, a dial module, the Rev C circuit board, firmware, a parts viewer, and printer and board ZIPs to download.
- The Technical Reference adds byte layouts, real-file examples and a utility architecture chapter, and the Ultimate Owner's Guide shows how to write a DOS installation batch file.

### Changed

- The Thank-You Parade is rebuilt as version 2.0, with a parade-poster look, a rival band and a level played as Marge the crop duster pilot.
- Dialogs fit a phone screen without scrolling, and New game keeps its three starts in view on a phone held sideways.
- Pages open with less to download, and the site and its offline copy are smaller: Starter ZIPs share the DOS player and are put back together in the browser, byte for byte the same.
- THE CHEZ makes the factory car's ka-chunk into 24 V, then 48 V as it pulls away, and coasts with a clunk and a fading whine, even past the desk window.
- The 3D-print trinkets and the phone kit download about two thirds less: the site sends small packed files and makes each STL, 3MF and the printer-parts ZIP in the browser when you save it.

### Fixed

- The home page's games list works by keyboard, focus moves to a new page's heading, and dialogs announce their titles to screen readers.
- My games no longer fills with "(damaged copy)" games.
- The mug of pens on the 1994 desk sits on the desk top again instead of hanging over the wall.
- Undo and Redo keep what they should in the background, monster, character, sample and music editors, and the music editor no longer freezes on a big song.

### Removed

- The desk PC's empty expansion bays, and the Corn Maze postcard's unused address lines.

Full list: [changes/release/pages/3.2.0.md](https://github.com/lewismoten/GameMaker-SandboxEdition/blob/pages-v3.2.0/changes/release/pages/3.2.0.md)

## [3.1.0] - 2026-11-02

The first tagged release of the site: the 1994 desk with its PC, BBSes, magazine and Library; today's web editor
for every Game-Maker file type; the web player; and the set of new games built in code.

### Added

- Colors That Work, a Starter for the Telling a Story group: two levels about colors everyone can tell apart. In the first, a plate that stings and a plate that is safe differ only by red against green, and a second picture on the level card shows how that can look to a player who sees red and green alike. In the second, spikes look like spikes and the safe path is light and striped, so it reads in any color.

- The web port of the PLAYGAME engine, playing original Game-Maker games and the new ones, with OPL2 and CMF music, voices and saves.
- The in-browser editor for maps, blocks, characters, monsters, palettes, pictures, animations, sounds, music, recordings, text screens, slide shows and the game integrator, with undo, a Level check and a walkthrough bot.
- The 1994 desk: a PC running DOS and Game-Maker, two BBSes, the magazine TURBO BUTTON, The Ultimate Owner's Guide, the Library, the cast page and felt eggs to find.
- More than twenty new games built in code, each with its manual, printables, box art, walkthrough and tour.
- Exports to a web page, a DOS ZIP and native players, and opening a game another site links to.
- Achievements, a guided tour with voice clips, and offline use as an installable app.
