# `desk/`: the 1994 desk

Part of the [source map](../README.md).

The 1994 desk. `desk.ts` puts it together and works it: the disks, the
boxes, the catalog and what the address asks for (`deskRestore.ts`). `deskScene.ts` draws the
scene (`stage.ts` scales it to the window); `deskPc.ts` switches the PC on and off and loads its
insides; `deskShelf.ts` fills the shelf, `deskGmBox.ts` opens the Game-Maker box, `deskLook.ts`
points out a prop, and `wallTrophies.ts` hangs the eggs and trophies once earned, and the framed certificate above the wall phone once a graduation is finished (`diploma/`: `frameLevel.ts` picks the level and frame, `diplomaArt.ts` draws it, `diplomaSpot.ts` hangs it, follows new honors and opens the Honors tab).
`overlayFocus.ts` moves the keyboard into an overlay and back. `wallTrophies.ts` also puts Teddy's Forte Locks toy safe ([`fortKnox/`](../fortKnox/README.md)) at the desk's right end after a catalog win, including the zero left by games with no money counter. Its combination is scribbled in pencil on the sticky note under the phone (`room/phoneNotes.ts`). `deskTour.ts` is the guided tour's BBS chapter on this PC: it
calls The Sandbox BBS and types for the tour, then puts back what the call changed.

- [`buddy/`](buddy/README.md): Scoot, the desk buddy, a pocket LCD toy on the desk top between the PC and the Game-Maker box that reacts to games, badges and certificates and opens a card of what it has seen.
- `pc/machineSettings.ts` and `pc/machineDialog.ts`: the keyboard-accessible MHz button opens remembered CPU and hard-drive timing choices. CPU presets reuse the engine’s PS/1, 286, 386 and 486 work estimates; Fast keeps the original desk speed. TURBO off slows CPU work to the 8 MHz readout, and on restores the chosen CPU. Disk presets independently set voice-read and scene-load costs for every game on the PC (including games opened from A: or D:); TURBO leaves disk timing alone. The PS/1 keeps its existing voice-heap limit. Hard-drive capacity, floppy type/speed and total RAM are omitted: capacity is not enforced, floppy timing currently only animates lights/sounds, and extended RAM has no engine effect. These limits are stated in the dialog. Settings use `gm-desk-machine` in local storage; TURBO still starts on each visit.

The dialog also switches the drive sounds (`pc/driveSwitches.ts`): All drive sounds, then Spin (motors), Heads (seek test, recalibration, parking, the CD's focus) and Read/write (the steps, knocks and clacks of reads) for the hard drive, CD-ROM and floppy, all on by default. `driveSounds.ts` and `bootSounds.ts` check each part's switch before scheduling it; disk seating, eject and tray sounds follow All drive sounds, the case fan is no drive, and Sound effects off still silences everything. Settings use `gm-desk-machine` in local storage; TURBO still starts on each visit.
- `ejectRule.ts`: drive A:'s eject button works any time but while A:'s light is on.
- `missingDisk.ts`: what the desk says when its address asks for a disk the box doesn't hold; a Starter or graduation is pointed to the Sample Disk or the CD's SAMPLES folder.
- `pc/`: the case (`caseParts.ts`: the drive cage as one touching stack, all in mm from one `--mm` factor: the bright white CD-ROM and the 3.5" floppy (laid flat, KELVO, a made-up maker) side by side with their tops aligned, no empty expansion bays; POWER, TURBO and H.D.D. as a row of lights above the control strip with the hard disk's flickering one; `caseFace.ts`: the drawn key lock (open and closed padlocks, padlocks above it, the red arrow along the 45 degree notch toward the open one) and the SANDBOX badge with its sand castle; RESET, round and right of TURBO, reboots a PC that is on, `Computer.reset`; the TURBO button sits beside the MHz display), the CRT (`crtWarp.ts`; `screenGlow.ts` lights the bezel with

  `crtOff.css` collapses it at switch-off), the fans whining up and the drives the boot reads (floppy seek test,

- `boxes/`: the retail boxes and their shelf (`shelf.ts`, `shelfLayout.ts`; each box is drawn when its slot nears the screen, its spine a small canvas from `boxSpine.ts`, so the desk's load does not grow with the games), turned in the box
  viewer (`boxViewer.ts`). The box in the viewer is kept in the address (`boxAddress.ts`): `#/desk?open=box:<game id>`, with `&turn=<y>,<x>,<zoom>` once turned by hand. Opening pushes a history entry (Back closes the viewer), stepping along the shelf replaces it; a reload or shared link opens it again, but only for a box on this visitor's shelf (an unknown id, or a secret game not yet revealed, opens nothing and is dropped from the address). The manual box opened from it ("Everything in the box", `manual/gameManual.ts`) has a **Game** picker first in its bar (`manual/printables/gamePicker.ts`): a labeled select plus ◀ ▶ (and the `[` and `]` keys; the arrows and PageUp/PageDown stay with the reader). It lists the shelf's boxes in shelf order (secret games only once revealed), swaps to that game's box at the same kind of piece, and the desk's `open=manual:<id>` follows. A game's box (`gameBox.ts`) is drawn from the game itself
  (`boxFacts.ts`, its play hours from its walkthrough in `playtime.ts`) with `boxPaint.ts`,
  `boxSeals.ts`, shelf wear (`boxWear.ts`), a glow under the black light (`boxUv.ts`) and a
  painted cover (`covers/<id>.ts`, with the brushes in `covers/paint.ts`); what came in it is
  `gameBoxContents.ts`, `boxList.ts` and `printContext.ts`. `gmBox.ts` is the Game-Maker box.
  **Feelies** (the 1980s-90s word for a game box's physical extras) are a game's plastic trinkets, laser kits and the phone kit, listed in `feelies.ts` (`loadFeelies`, from the manifests below; a secret game has none). Under the turning box `trinketShelf.ts` shows only a labeled group "Feelies": one thumbnail button each (`aria-label` is its name, 76 px), a real render drawn once by `feelieThumbs.ts` with the same WebGL viewers (the nickel uses its own `front.png`), and no text or file links. Beside them sit the box's paper feelies (a code wheel, the fingerprint card, the spyglass; `BoxItem.feelie`, set in `manual/printables/items.ts`) as real renders of their first sheet (`paperFeelies.ts`), each opening the box's contents at that piece; a "What are feelies?" button under the strip opens a short note on what feelies are. Choosing a 3D one takes the box's place with that feelie as a slowly turning 3D model (drag, arrow keys, wheel or pinch to zoom, Home to reset); choosing it again, or Esc (`boxViewer.ts` hands the first Esc to the strip), brings the box back, and PageUp/PageDown change feelie. `feelieModels.ts` builds each model's view: a trinket's STL in plastic (`trinketGl.ts`, colors from its preview STL), the wooden nickel as a 38.1 mm by 3 mm wood coin with a reeded rim and its front and back PNGs on the faces (`coinModel.ts`), and the Stage Box as the closed outer box of six 3 mm panels from the manifest's part sizes, each outside face carrying the engraving cropped from its sheet's preview (`stageBoxModel.ts`; the mechanism inside is not drawn). Both laser models use `texturedGl.ts`, the sibling of `trinketGl.ts` for textured pieces; `trinketTurntable.ts` shows either (`showModel`), spinning, with zoom. `trinketData.ts` reads the trinket manifests and STL meshes (shared with the Library's toy box, `library/toyBox.ts`) and `trinketSnap.ts` draws a trinket once as a still PNG for lists. `laserKits.ts` reads `public/laser/<id>/patch/manifest.json` (Maximus's embroidered patch, `kind: "patch"`, `id: "embroidery-patch"`, drawn as a disc with a raised satin rim by `patchModel.ts`; built by `games/maximus/patch/build.ts`), `public/laser/<id>/manifest.json` and `public/laser/<id>/stage-box/manifest.json` (a game can hold both kits; written by `games/kit/laserNickels.ts` and `games/sleuth/laser/stageBox.ts`): the wooden nickel (`key: "laser-nickel"`) is worded in `kitOf`; any other kit adds a `kit: { title, summary, previews, downloads }` entry (and, to be drawn in 3D as a box, `sheets` with each part's place). Tests: `feelies.test.ts`, `laserKits.test.ts`.
  The words and files of each feelie are not here but in the box's "What's in the box" list (`gameBoxContents.ts`, `manual/printables/feelieItems.ts`; see that folder's README): each trinket and laser kit a piece with its blurb and downloads, and the phone a build book and a files page.
  `boxes/phone/` is The Phone of the Future kit (year2000's box only), the last feelie, loaded with its first look. `phoneData.ts` parses the committed `games/year2000/phone/viewer/parts.json` and `parts.bin` (served packed from `public/trinkets/phone/` as `parts.bin.pack`, by `games/kit/phoneDownloads.ts`) and gives each part a group (shell, dial, board, components, battery; a part's own `group` wins); `phoneExplode.ts` works out each stack's explode offset along the height from the parts' own extents (so shell, dial and button-cap parts added to the data need no code); `phoneMesh.ts` and `phoneGl.ts` draw them with the trinket shader; `phoneViewer.ts` floats the phone like the others and adds a small panel over it: the build switch (No. 2 Junior Lab, the 99 x 99 mm cheap board, and No. 1 Deluxe, the 73.66 x 175.26 mm board; each has its own `parts.json` and `parts.bin`, the deluxe set in `public/trinkets/phone/deluxe/`), the four views (assembled, exploded, electronics, bare board), the explode slider and a one-line size in mm and inches. Both builds are drawn at the same scale (`createPhoneView`'s `scaleWith`). Its downloads (the printer parts, zipped in the browser from their pack by `phonePrinterZip`, a ZIP of the circuit board files, the parts lists, the README the build book reads) are made at build time by `games/kit/phoneDownloads.ts` into `public/trinkets/phone/`. Tests: `phoneData.test.ts`.

The paper-stack menu opens either TURBO BUTTON issue explicitly. Passive desk navigation clears missing disk hints silently; explicit PC actions still explain missing disks. The disk box filters by title or author without changing its collections, and the Sample Disk uses a neutral label. The Feelies strip links to build instructions and downloads in the box contents.
