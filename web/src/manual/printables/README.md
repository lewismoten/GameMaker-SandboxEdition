# What's in the box: manuals and printables

Every Sandbox Software game (`games/<id>/` with `about.json`, `manual.json` and `disk/`) opens
its manual with `openGameManual(entry, cover?)` (`../gameManual.ts`). The viewer shows:

- **The manual**: `manual.json` pages flowed onto 5.5 x 8.5 inch booklet pages (long chapters
  continue on extra pages automatically), padded with Notes and Hall of Fame pages to whole
  folded sheets, with its own front cover (`manualCover.ts`: the title in the box's colors, the
  manual's name, a screenshot and the hero), a back cover and two staples in the center spread. Any `P.O. Box` address gets a printed note that it is fictional.
- **What's in the box**: a tray of paper extras. Each one can be read on screen and printed.

Printing (🖨 Print…): this piece, the manual as a booklet (two pages a side on letter paper
turned sideways: print double-sided, fold the stack and staple the fold), or everything. The
browser's own Print command prints the manual page by page.

A game with no manual (the Bard of Bizarre's song-games) gets a 🗺 Maps button instead, which
opens the same viewer with only the World Map and Level Maps (`openGameMaps`).

## games/&lt;id&gt;/printables.json

`news` is the game's issue of The Sandbox Scoop, written for the month it shipped. The first story leads the front page under the box shot; then a headline starting `Since last issue:` looks back at the game before, `In the workshop:` teases the next one by name (the last issue names none), `Around the Sandbox:` carries the studio's running story and `Letters:` the mailbag. `scoopIssues.test.ts` checks the order and that no issue names a secret game. After changing a headline or adding a story, run `npx vite-node scripts/scoopDates.ts` (from `web/`) for the RSS feed. (`everywhere` is no longer used.) All fields are optional. Types: `types.ts` (`PrintablesFile`).

```json
{
  "pitch": "One line for the catalog.",
  "skip": ["bbs"],
  "keys": { "Space": "Fire", "Z": "Plasma Lance" },
  "news": [{ "head": "In the workshop: a story for The Sandbox Scoop", "html": "<p>Simple HTML.</p>" }],
  "stickers": [
    { "text": "GOOD DOG", "sub": "small line", "shape": "badge", "color": "#b3121a", "ink": "#fff" },
    { "text": "HONK!", "sub": "PARK GOOSE", "shape": "circle", "color": "#ffd23f", "ink": "#2a1408", "sprite": { "blocks": "park.mbl", "pal": "park.pal", "grid": [[31]] } },
    { "text": "TEDDY", "shape": "badge", "hero": { "chr": "teddy.chr", "cbl": "teddy.cbl", "pal": "backyard.pal" } }
  ],
  "items": [ ...in tray order, see below... ],
  "worldMap": { "groups": [{ "scenes": [4, "5-8"], "name": "Nowish", "labels": ["Grandma Pim", "Doc Leo"] }] }
}
```

`worldMap.groups` gathers scenes into one place on the World Map: the first scene is the place, the rest fold into it. A scene is a number, a range (`"5-8"`) or a name only one scene has; `name` renames the place and `labels` replaces the list of folded scenes on its land. Scenes under an all-black palette fold on their own, so a game only needs groups for other scenes that aren't places (Final Curtain's interview rooms, for one).

### World Map themes and layout

The theme comes from the game's title and blurb (`worldMapThemes.ts`, first match wins), or from `HINTS` there, keyed by game id, where the words would mislead (Ohm Sweet Ohm's blurb says "circuit boards", but its map is the house):

| theme     | for                          | ground (`worldMapGround.ts`, `worldMapIndoors.ts`)                                  | paths (`worldMapWays.ts`)  |
| --------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| `space`   | a galaxy, planets            | a starfield with a chart's rings; each region (a sector) a nebula; worlds in orbits | warp lanes                 |
| `board`   | inside a computer            | a green circuit board, each region outlined in white, little parts between          | copper traces with pads    |
| `grid`    | inside a renderer            | a draftsman's blue grid, regions boxed, spheres and polygons                        | pen lines                  |
| `library` | among books                  | a floor plan: each region a room with walls, shelves, doors where halls leave       | aisles                     |
| `rooms`   | indoors (a house, a theater) | the same floor plan with furniture                                                  | halls                      |
| `town`    | round town, along a road     | city blocks, a lot for each place, houses and trees                                 | streets with a center line |
| `land`    | anything else                | one land in a sea, a country round each place in its picture's colors, its terrain  | roads                      |

The layout (`worldMapPlan.ts`) tries arrangements of the places on a grid of cells, from the Game Integrator's own (`layoutCells.ts`: the game as a tree across or down, play order snaking along rows, hubs over their dead ends) and `worldMapBands.ts` (each hub over a block of its dead ends, side by side in bands, the way between them along the top: Maximus's sectors), judges them with straight lines (crossings, length, a region's places kept together, a shape near a sheet turned sideways) and routes the best few for real (`worldMapRoute.ts`: A* on a grid a tenth of an inch apart, straight runs across and down, leaving a place from the top or sides of its picture, never under another place, a path from the same place welcome to share a lane like a road that forks). The plan with the fewest crossings and the shortest, straightest paths wins. `worldMapMeasure.ts` counts crossings and paths under places; `worldMapPlan.test.ts` holds every game to none under a place and at most one crossing.

A sticker's `sprite` is rows of block numbers from a `.mbl`, `.cbl` or `.bbl` file (-1 leaves a cell empty); `hero` draws a character standing, from his `.chr` file. Shapes: `cut` (cut out round the picture's own outline, its words on a plate under it), `circle`, `rect`, `star` (a seal), `badge`, `heart`, `burst` (a comic-book bang) and `bubble` (a speech balloon). A background thing drawn over ground (a tin heart, an egg, THE CHEZ) names that ground block in `sprite.under` (or a grid of them, or `"edge"` for speckled ground) so the ground is cut away; `sprite.flip` faces it the other way. `px` sets a die-cut's size (each pixel prints that many 1/150 inch, so it stays crisp; raised if the sticker would be under the least size); every character also comes as a filler mini in the sheet's gaps unless `noMini` (or unless it would be over 1.6 in long at the least size). The sheet adds the title on a floppy, a screenshot on a TV, I ♥ SHAREWARE and the Sandbox Software mark (`sandboxMark.ts`), packs everything tight (`stickerArt.ts`, `stickerGeom.ts`) and, if it spills onto a second sheet, draws the die-cuts a step smaller. The hero sticker also goes on the game's disk label.

**Sticker rules** (from Cricut's Print Then Cut guidance and die-cut printers' specs; tests in `stickerArt.test.ts`):

- **Area:** 6.75 x 9.25 in (`CUT_W`, `CUT_H`), the Print Then Cut area every machine takes on letter paper, clear of Design Space's sensor marks. Every sticker's bleed stays inside it. The sheet is 8 x 10.5 in, so the viewer prints it at actual size inside a printer's margins.
- **Size:** every sticker at least 1 in across at its narrowest (`MIN_SIZE`); the layout refuses a smaller one.
- **Bleed:** 1/16 in (`BLEED`). Each sticker's border or background color runs past its cut line, so a cut a little off lands on color, never white paper.
- **Spacing:** 1/4 in between cut lines (`GAP`): both bleeds and white between them.
- **Die-cut border:** 1/8 in (`CUT_OFFSET`) of the sticker's color round its art, with a thin white line hugging the art. The cut line is that offset closed: inner corners rounded (at least 0.1 in) and narrow gaps filled, parts that stand apart bridged into one piece, and the traced line smoothed, so the blade follows one smooth shape.
- **Keylines** on shaped stickers sit 0.1 in inside the cut, clear of a cut a little off.
- **Words:** a small line that would print under 6 pt is left off; seals and bangs keep their words inside their notches.
- **On paper:** the bleed runs past each cut line. The dashed cut line (white dashes on a dark hairline, plain on any color) is drawn only when the screen's Show cut guides is ticked, for scissors and glue: a cutting machine's cut a little off would leave dashes showing.

Every item has `kind`, `title` (tray name), optional `note` (tray subtitle) and `promised: true`
when the manual promises it to registered players (it gets a REG seal). If the manual promises
something with registration, add it here so it is in the box.

A **map window** (`MapPanel`) is `{ "map": "x.map", "x": 0, "y": 0, "w": 16, "h": 10, "caption": "..." }`
in blocks; it is drawn from the game's real blocks and monsters.
