# Pick It Up: tools

- `check.ts`: the bot plays both levels. `route.ts` asserts what each sign teaches: in level 1, P takes each of three crates into the pack (the cell turns to sky), a jump at the six-block wall fails, and D then the pack's first place puts a crate down where Kit stands; after one and after two crates a jump still falls short, and after three Kit reaches the top; in level 2, Enter with an empty pack shrugs (sequence 17), P takes the flame and the star in order, Enter with both throws the flame (sequence 14) and melts the frosty grump, a flame at the far gumdrop falls short, D puts the flame down, Enter then throws the star (15) and pops the gumdrop, and P takes the flame back. Every coin is taken and no life is lost.
- `walkthrough.ts`, `gendemo.ts`: re-record the walkthrough and the title demo. The demo is both levels as one unbroken list of keys (the demo recorder counts game ticks across scene changes), stopped before the last door.
- `demoCheck.ts`: plays the built `demo.rec` on the real engine and checks it enters scenes 2 and 3, presses P and D enough to take and put down the crates, the flame and the star, and has no game over.
- `preview.ts`: PNGs of the blocks, the hero, each level and every picture. `shots.ts`: pictures of the lessons for the progress shots.

## What the engine does here, and what it does not

A block with **Object** ticked is picked up by the pick-up key (sequence 3) instead of being touched. `PickupBloc` in `PLAYGAME.C` takes the first object the hero overlaps into the next free place of the pack (ten places, `ci.inv`), and `reallyCheckTouch` then runs its "touch becomes" rule, so a crate leaves sky behind. The put-down key (sequence 4) opens the pack screen (`DropBloc`: arrows, Enter, Esc) and writes the chosen block number into the map at the hero's own place, closing the gap in the pack.

A sequence can be tied to a block (its `bloc`, "Block required in inventory"). `StartSceneInits` switches a tied sequence off, and `checkseq_fortie` switches it on while its block is in the pack and off again when it is put down. When a key is pressed, `SimKeyPress` starts the first sequence on it that is switched on, so the order of the list is the only "selection": a flame before a star throws the flame while both are carried. The headless sim cannot see the pack screen's keys, so `GameSim.dropSlot` names the place the picker answers with and the sim writes the Right, Down and Enter presses a person would make into the walkthrough; the replay (`SegmentRecorder.pollOnce`) hands them to the picker one tick apart, as a demo made in DOS does.

A thrown weapon is a monster born by a frame whose second picture is `MONBL`; the stronger one in a collision survives, a thrown one is always used up, and its lifetime (`end`, in ticks) sets its reach: here a flame flies 10 ticks at 4 pixels (two blocks) and a star 30 (six). A solid block at the hero's height stops a throw, so the spikes (which stand at his height) are not solid: they sting once and lie flat. A solid spike block would sting Kit every tick he stood on it (the engine touches the cell below him before gravity is stopped by it).
