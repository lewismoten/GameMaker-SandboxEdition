"Yes," says Oliver Pike at
last. "It was my play."

He wrote THE RAVEN'S BRIDE in
1931 and took it to Victor
Crane, who laughed him out of
the office, then put his own
name on it five years later.

On opening night Pike took his
umbrella from the cloakroom at
9:05. Inside it was an antique
dagger, the twin of the trick
prop.


He went down the aisle from
F-9, through the pit gate and
the pit door, under the stage
and up the trap stairs into
the empty stage-right wing.

He put the real dagger on the
prop table and slid the trick
one into his umbrella. His
ticket stub fell under the
table.

Then, hearing the crew, he
stood the umbrella in the old
prop vault beyond the trap
room and was back in his seat
for the bell.

At 9:48 Lila Fontaine did the
rest without knowing it.












The Paragon opens again on
Tuesday with Edgar Bliss as
Count Corvin. The program, and
a Courier correction, say the
play is by Oliver Pike.

Lila Fontaine's contract is
torn up. Felix Morrow keeps
his job. Agatha pays the bank.

And Inspector Ada Penn goes
home, as the sun comes up over
Port Ellery, to sleep until
the evening papers.

CASE CLOSED.
