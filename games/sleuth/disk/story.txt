Port Ellery, Saturday,
October 31, 1936.

Opening night at the Paragon
Theatre: THE RAVEN'S BRIDE, a
new melodrama written by,
directed by and starring the
Paragon's owner, Victor Crane.

Six hundred people watched Act
III. As the bride Rowena, Lila
Fontaine stabbed the wicked
Count Corvin with a trick
dagger, the kind whose blade
slides into the handle.

Victor Crane fell, as he did
at every rehearsal.

This time he did not get up.












The dagger in his heart was
not the trick dagger. It was
real: an antique blade with a
raven's head on the pommel, as
sharp as the day it was made.

The audience has gone home.
The cast and crew are held in
the green room. The rain has
not stopped.

At half past ten, a police car
pulls up under the marquee and
Inspector Ada Penn of Homicide
steps out.

She has until dawn.
