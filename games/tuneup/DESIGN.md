# Tune-Up: design

A Sandbox Software game. Version 2 rebuilds the first version (Teddy fetching instruments
across thirteen neighborhoods) as a tune-up of THE CHEZ by ear. It is built in phases; this
file says what is built.

## Story

It's the morning of the Clown Club parade, and THE CHEZ is meant to lead it. Overnight, Gwinn
buried Doc Leo's toolbox in the yard and tipped the Clown Club's instrument trunk in through the
door window (THE CHEZ has no hood). With no wrenches, Doc Leo does his shrinking trick and tunes the car up by ear. Sir
Reginald III, his rubber chicken, does the fiddly bits (he fits where Doc Leo can't), and the
two of them talk every repair over. Teddy rides along in the front passenger seat.

Levels 1-4 are Sir Reginald Cluckington III's dream (he was talking in his sleep): a gas
engine THE CHEZ never had. After Oil's Well he swims the oil pipe (a scene nobody steers), and
Doc Leo's card wakes him: THE CHEZ is electric (no exhaust, no spark plugs, no engine oil; the
only oil is in the rear differential), so the dream can be dismissed. The chicken blames
battery fumes; Doc Leo says the batteries are lithium ion now (a deliberate time slip, logged in
the timeline), and the chicken tells the player such car batteries are years off. What's really
wrong: THE CHEZ has no radio, so the real tune-up (levels 5 on) builds toward an AM radio with an
8-track. Every car fact from level 5 on is from the car's blog, thechez.car.blog. After Pump Up
the Volume a balloon pops, he lays an egg, and from then on every card names her Lady Regina
III, formally corrected (*CORRECTION: Lady Regina Cluckington III). She wears a tiara in front of her comb, her name is pink on the cards, and before The Rain Delay a card has her keep the egg on Doc Leo's hat. (PLAYGAME can't show a card only when the felt egg wasn't found, so that card always plays and never mentions finding anything.)

## How it's made

- **Palettes** (`src/art/palette.ts`): a fixed half (the Teddy finale's ramps, Doc Leo's, the
  chicken's) and seven scenery roles each scene colors from a theme, as maximus does. One block
  drawn in roles is the garage at dawn or the engine bay lit blue-white, brass or amber.
- **Block sets** (`src/blocks/sets.ts`): GARAGE, ENGINE, CURB, CAB and PARADE base sets. A
  level's .BBL starts with its base set in the same slots and adds its own blocks.
- **Doc Leo** (`src/sprites/leo.ts`) is the hobo clown: chewed gray fedora, his own face paint,
  wire glasses, a patched navy corduroy jacket and a red shop rag (the poses follow CERTIFIED RAT
  TICKLER's drawing, without its lab coat). Three character files: LEO (walk, hop, climb, a reach that
  repeats while Space is held), PIANO (one reach a press) and OIL (E G B D F each fire a drop into
  their own row, no hopping).
- **The Timing Beat** (`src/levels/beat.ts`): a player piano, one picture in all three verses.
  The roll is the top row: six cells of paper (two wider than the four keys), a thin ragged edge
  block each side, and the crank wheel turning it at the left. Its holes feed glass tubes down
  to four keys; boop a key as its note lands. A bellows breathes under the wheel and four organ
  pipes on the right speak each lane's note as it lands.
  - A tube cell `d` cells above its key shows a ring of blocks started at member `d - r`, so lanes
    whose notes are one pattern turned share a ring. Keys judge the timing by the frame on show.
    Each lane's hit is its own kind of change, so its own pitch; each verse counts on its own
    counter.
  - The roll's cell is just the tube's top cell under the same rule, so nothing in the timetable
    moves (the keys, the song, the bot's route). Its ring draws the paper scrolled 5 pixels a step
    (a turn of the wheel every 4 steps) and each hole over the 4 steps before its note drops: a
    sliver at the roll's crest that grows as it comes toward you, full size on the step the note
    enters the tube. A ring shared by cells started at different members can't scroll them
    together, so each roll ring also carries the paper's turn (a cell whose turn differs gets its
    own ring). Plain paper, the edges, the wheel and the bellows are 4-step rings started in step.
  - Every tube reaches the roll, so verse 3's crossover is gone (its tubes run straight; double
    time is its trouble). Rings as long as a pattern takes to repeat (verse 2's first lane, every 8
    steps) keep verse 2 under PLAYGAME's 150 blocks.
- **Oil's Well** (`src/levels/oil.ts`): a drop (power 60) turns a Squeak (50) into a note (60)
  that heads for Doc Leo; its monster block counts it on touch. Waves are blocks that give
  birth once on a timetable.
- **Timers belong to block numbers, not cells.** PLAYGAME runs every block's timer for all
  blocks of its number at once, from the scene's start, so a touched block can't start its own
  countdown. Balloons are rings that breathe on their own (each balloon starts at its own
  point); a squeaky toy squeaks once and stays squashed. PLAYGAME also changes only blocks
  wholly on screen, so nothing touchable sits on a level's top row.
- **Teddy** (`src/heroes.ts`) is the trilogy's corgi drawing, one block tall, with four tricks
  that each fire a sparkle on a power ladder (`src/realMonsters.ts`). **Nobody** is a clear
  block with no keys, for the swim.
- **Rings on the song's beat** (levels 10-13): a block's ring starts with the scene, as its song
  does, so a ring whose frames add up to whole beats keeps time with the music: the wiper blades,
  the horn board, the gear teeth, the door's clock and the radio dial's needle are all rings. A
  ring that should run late (the upper tooth, a beat behind) is its own ring of blocks, not a
  later frame of the same one.
- **Touches**: PLAYGAME touches the cells at a move's destination before solids stop it, so a
  reach up into a solid pad touches it, and standing on a block touches it every tick (gravity
  tries to move the hero down). So nothing that costs points is ever stood on; the horn pads are
  overhead, and a wrong one blows its raspberry only when reached.
- **Counters** for doors in levels 10-13 reuse the fans' and the tapes' counters, which their
  doors spent to nothing; counters a gate leaves something in (the Timing Beat's) are never reused
  for a door.
- **The choir swell** (`src/sound/realSongs2.ts` `choirSong`): rhythm mode off for all nine FM
  voices, each pair a voice and its vibrato twin (`kit/songFx` `detuned`), coming in a bar apart,
  louder by program changes (`sweep`, `swell`); the ninth sings the tune.
- **Views**: a level's view is centered on where Doc Leo starts and never scrolls up or down,
  so every level starts him at (8, 5) in mid air and he drops to the floor (THE CHEZ starts
  at row 5 too, nine blocks from the right end, and drives left).

- **THE CHEZ as the hero** (`src/sprites/chezHero.ts`): the shared side view at half scale,
  facing left (the passenger side, Teddy in the window). A hero is one block wide and its top
  block here is clear, so only the car's own block touches. Left is a momentum sequence (its
  frames add to the car's momentum, as Jet Driver's Up does); with no momentum sequence running
  the momentum decays to 5/6 twice a tick, so letting go coasts to a stop; Right holds still (a
  brake); Down backs up; Space toots and bounces 6 pixels, a reach into the row above.
- **Signals** (`src/levels/drive.ts`): a light's head and the pole's foot are two rings of 12
  blocks with the same timings (a bar of the countdown each); every light shares them, started
  at its own member. Touches use the shown block, so the foot fines on red (points only, the
  score-down slot plays the camera and siren) and turns into the camera's smiley card, or toots
  on green and turns plain; one touch a corner. Stop signs are a ring of four: three dots
  (fines), then GO (a pass).
- **The parade's cue lamps** (`src/levels/parade.ts`) are solid underneath in the row over the
  car; the bounce touches them. Both kinds count on the stations counter (spent to nothing by
  level 13's door), and the ribbon spends four.
